Hello! I am **Haoyang (Stan) Xie**, a fourth-year PhD student in the Kenneth C. Griffin Department of Economics at the University of Chicago. Previously, I was a Research Professional at the Becker Friedman Institute. I received my M.A. from the University of Chicago and my B.A. from Peking University.

My research interests include political economy, environmental economics, and development economics, with a focus on Africa (and its ties with China).

## Research Highlights

### Agents Pulled Over Drivers, Often Using Brute Force

<figure class="research-figure">
  <img src="{{ '/images/sentiment.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Research figure on traffic police interactions with drivers and the use of force" width="1883" height="1332" loading="lazy" decoding="async">
</figure>

A large share of unofficial police income is generated through a “quota scheme”: managers at police stations ask agents posted at street intersections to escort drivers to the stations, where managers extract bribes. Experimentally decreasing the quota worsens the agency’s ability to manage traffic without improving its ability to enforce the traffic code.

### Kickbacks Motivated by Distributional Concerns

<figure class="research-figure">
  <img src="{{ '/images/figure-model.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Model figure illustrating supervisor kickbacks and distributional concerns" width="700" height="376" loading="lazy" decoding="async">
</figure>

We conduct a within-individual randomized cash transfer in the Democratic Republic of the Congo’s traffic police agency to study supervisor kickbacks. We present the first evidence of side payments from state officials to their supervisors. Our findings suggest that collusion between supervisors and agents can destroy the benefits of traditional anti-corruption policies.

### Decay of State Capacity over Distance

<figure class="research-figure research-figure--square">
  <img src="{{ '/images/capacity-figure.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Research figure showing how estimated state capacity varies over distance" width="1428" height="1428" loading="lazy" decoding="async">
</figure>

Motivated by Sanchez de la Sierra (2021), I structurally quantify a measure of state capacity and use it to characterize how state capacity varies over space. Each kilometer away from a state office decreases state capacity by 5%. These findings build on the concept of legal capacity in the theoretical literature and show that state weakness is heterogeneous over space.
